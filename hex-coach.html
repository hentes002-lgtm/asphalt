The base view shows the name and gift chance. Turn on Statistics view for the !, opens and rules-out numbers (gift + ! + opens always add up to 100). Hover a tile (tap and hold on a phone) for the same numbers in words. Tick Heatmap in the corner of the advice box to colour every tile by how many clicks it takes to finish the board if you click it now: bright green = the Advisor's pick, darker = more clicks, with the difference (+0.06 and so on) on each tile. The colours are relative to the current board, so even small differences show. With a mouse, the Advisor's pick is outlined in green while you hover over the board, even when the advice is hidden, and every tooltip shows how it compares.

  1. Tap one ✕ tile from your game. The second one is placed automatically, since the game always puts it at the 180° mirror position.
  2. Set-up: copy any tiles already open in your game by tapping them here: 1× opened, 2× !, 3× gift, 4× hidden again. New game? Skip it. Then tap 2 · Coaching in the switch under the board. You can go back any time with 1 · Set up.
  3. The Advisor is on from the start (Hide advice switches it off): it plays every possible gift layout to the end and turns the click that finishes the board in the fewest clicks on average green. Usually that's the highest chance, but not always. Several green tiles are equally good. Click it in your game.
  4. Tap the same tile here and pick what your game showed: Gift, ! or Opened. Each button shows how likely that result was.
  5. Gift found: the ! tiles next to it flash as !?. Say whether each still shows ! or turned plain. When the answer is already certain, the coach fills it in for you, flashes the tile and tells you what it filled in.
  6. Save keeps the board on this device only (browser storage: no cookies, no tracking, nothing sent). It lights up when there's unsaved progress, and only saves when you tap it. Next time you open the page, it offers to load the saved board. Delete removes it.
  7. Wrong entry? Undo (Ctrl+Z) and Redo (Ctrl+Y) step through every entry, including set-up taps.
  8. Practice (top switch): a random board that answers by itself. On a phone, the first tap shows the possibilities, the second opens the tile.

For the curious: how the Advisor decides, and how its results were measured. One click always costs one decoder, so "clicks" and "decoders" mean the same here.

Game model

  • 42 tiles in rows of 8-9-8-9-8. Two ✕ tiles start open on inner tiles; the second is always the 180° mirror of the first, so there are 10 possible ✕ pairs. 3 gifts sit on any 3 of the other 40 tiles, uniformly at random.
  • A click shows a gift, a ! (no gift, but a hidden gift is next to it), or opens the tile together with its hidden neighbours. Tiles opened that way tell you nothing about their own neighbours.
  • A ! goes plain once every gift next to it is found.

How the Advisor picks

  • It lists every gift layout that still fits the board: 9,880 at the start (3 gifts on 40 tiles).
  • For every tile you could click, it follows every one of those layouts to the end of the game, letting a strong base strategy play the later clicks (highest chance, ties to the click that rules out the most layouts). That gives the exact expected number of clicks to finish for that tile.
  • It picks the tile with the lowest number. This is a one-step policy improvement, so on average it can never be worse than the base strategy.
  • The board has exactly 3 symmetries: the left-right mirror, the top-bottom mirror and the 180° turn (checked against every possible mapping). Whenever the board is symmetric, mirror-image tiles get the same value, so all equally good tiles turn green.
  • Mirror-image boards are exactly equally hard, but breaking ties in reading order plays some of them better than their twin. For those ✕ starts the Advisor thinks on the mirrored board and flips its answer back, so twin boards always score the same.
  • The 1st hint (for every ✕ pair) and the 2nd hint (after every possible first click) are precomputed, so both are instant, even on a phone. Later hints are calculated in your browser, in the background.

Where the gain comes from

Compared with the previous Advisor, this one saves 0.24 decoders per board (10.31 → 10.07). Improving one phase at a time shows where:

PhaseDecoders saved per board
First click0.020
Before the 1st gift0.167
Between the 1st and 2nd gift0.092
Last gift0.008
Playing mirror twins the same way0.006

The phases were measured separately, so they don't add up exactly to the total. For the last gift, exact perfect play is within about 0.001 of the Advisor, so the endgame is essentially solved.

Checks

  • The engine in this page was compared against an independent simulator: 113 game positions and 160 tile values on symmetric boards, 0 differences.
  • All precomputed 1st and 2nd hints match the live engine exactly.
  • Full games played automatically through this page, on desktop and on a simulated phone, with no errors.

Limitations

  • 10.07 is not proven optimal. True perfect play is at or below it, most likely only slightly.
  • All numbers assume the game places gifts uniformly at random. If it doesn't, the numbers shift.
